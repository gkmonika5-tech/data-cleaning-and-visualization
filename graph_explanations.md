# What each graph tells us

## Bar chart
Compares the mean average score of students by their parents' education. Scores go from 57.6 (High School) to 63.2 (Master's), so a better-educated home is linked with somewhat higher marks.

## Pie chart
Shows how the 500 students are spread over grades. Grades B and C are the most common (36.2% and 37.0%); only 7.2% reach grade A and 2.4% get grade F.

## Histogram
Shows the shape of the scores. The mean (58.9) and the median (58.5) are close, so the distribution is roughly bell-shaped and symmetric, with most students between 52 and 65 marks.

## Box plot
Compares the spread of scores for each school type. The box is the middle 50% of students and the line inside is the median. Private schools have the highest median (61.0) and Government the lowest (56.9); dots outside the whiskers are unusual students.

## Scatter plot
Each dot is a student. The points rise from left to right: more weekly study hours go with higher scores (correlation r = 0.71, a strong positive relationship). Students with extra classes tend to sit above those without.

## Correlation heatmap
Each cell is the correlation between two columns: +1 means they rise together, 0 means no relationship, -1 means one rises as the other falls. Study hours (r = 0.71) and previous score (r = 0.58) are linked with the average score; age has almost no link. The three subject scores are strongly related to each other.

## Before/after box plot
Before cleaning, study hours reach 150 per week (impossible for a student); after cleaning the maximum is 37.8. The extreme values were capped with the IQR rule, so the box plot is no longer stretched by outliers.
